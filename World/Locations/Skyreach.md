
_The Aerie of the Five Houses_

## Overview

Skyreach is built into the upper reaches of a mountain range so tall that clouds regularly drift below its lowest terraces. It isn't a village in the way outsiders picture one there's no central square, no farmland ringing its edges. Instead it's a series of carved ledges, bridges, and hollowed-out cliff faces stacked along the mountainside like a real aerie, connected by rope bridges, switchback stairs, and updraft channels the Aasimar use to glide between levels rather than climb them.

There is no agriculture to speak of at this altitude. Skyreach survives on what it hunts, what it trades from the beasts it brings down (hides, bones, feathers, rare monster parts sought after by lowland alchemists and smiths), and a careful trade relationship with a lowland town at the base of the mountain that the village calls simply **the Foothold.** Outsiders are rare this high up, and Skyreach prefers it that way visitors are tolerated, not welcomed.

## How Skyreach Functions

Skyreach isn't ruled so much as **organized.** Every person belongs to one of the Five Houses — Hawk, Falcon, Owl, Raven, Eagle — determined by bloodline, and each House manages its own training, its own hunting parties, and its own companion-rearing traditions. Houses specialize:

- **House Hawk** — scouts, trackers, skirmishers. First into the field, first to strike.
- **House Falcon** — speed and pursuit. They run down anything that tries to flee.
- **House Owl** — night hunters and watch-keepers. Skyreach's eyes after dark.
- **House Raven** — lore-keepers, healers, and messengers. Fewer hunters, more strategists.
- **House Eagle** — the heavy hitters. Larger companions, frontline fighters against the biggest game.

No House functions alone for long. Hunting parties are almost always mixed — a Hawk to scout, a Falcon to run it down, an Eagle to finish it, a Raven to keep everyone alive doing it.

### Leadership

Skyreach has no single ruler. It's governed by the **Council of Talons** — one elder chosen from each House, typically the most accomplished living hunter of that bloodline, who sits for life or until they can no longer fly the Long Circuit (the aerial patrol elders are expected to complete each season to prove their fitness to lead).

- **Vask Emberwing** _(House Eagle)_ — First Talon, the closest thing Skyreach has to a head of state. Broad-winged, silent unless he has something worth saying, chosen First Talon after leading the hunt that finally killed the Chalk Wyrm three decades ago. Older hunters still tell that story to frighten and inspire the young in the same breath.
- **Rhiane Hawk** _(House Hawk)_ — Kass's own great-aunt, and the elder most responsible for shaping how his House trains its scouts. Sharp-tongued, exacting, and the person in the village least impressed by Kass's ambitions — which hasn't stopped him from wanting her approval more than anyone else's.
- **Tallis Windrue** _(House Falcon)_ — Youngest of the Council, brought on more for his speed records than his patience. Popular with younger hunters, less trusted by the older Talons.
- **Corvane Ashqill** _(House Raven)_ — Keeper of Skyreach's oral histories and its dwindling written archive. If anyone in the village knows the true story behind the beast Kass wants to hunt, it's Corvane — and Corvane isn't telling.
- **Ossa Nightfeather** _(House Owl)_ — Runs the night watch and the Long Flight trials personally. Every hunter who's come of age in the last forty years has been judged, at some point, under her eye.

Below the Council, each House has its own **Wingmasters** — senior hunters who train the young and lead hunting parties day to day. Rhiane's most trusted Wingmaster, **Barrow Hawk**, was the one who actually paired Kass with Skree and oversaw much of his early training.

## Notable Landmarks

**The Roost** — Skyreach's largest carved hall, built into the mountain's central hollow. Where the Council of Talons meets, where the whole village gathers for feasts after a major hunt, and where the bones and hides of the village's greatest kills hang from the rafters as both trophy and warning.

**The Hatchery** — A sheltered, sun-facing terrace where companion eggs and chicks are raised alongside the village's children. Considered close to sacred; even outsiders granted access to Skyreach are rarely permitted here.

**The Long Perch** — The departure point for every hunter beginning their Long Flight. A narrow ledge jutting far out from the mountainside with nothing below it but open air for a thousand feet — tradition holds that you don't truly begin your Flight until you've jumped from it, companion at your side, and caught the wind on your own.

**The Ashfall Cliffs** — A blackened, still-scarred stretch of the mountain a half-day's flight from the village proper. Something burned it decades before Kass was born, and the elders don't speak of what. It's the place most closely tied to the legend of the beast Kass has set his sights on — and the one place in Skyreach's territory that hunting parties are still forbidden to approach alone.

**The Bone Spire** — A natural stone spire near the Roost, ringed with the skulls and horns of the largest beasts Skyreach has ever brought down. Every hunter who completes their first true monster kill earns the right to add something to it. It's more than half-full. Kass has already picked out the empty space near the top where he intends his contribution to go.

**The Foothold** — The lowland trading town at the base of the mountain, technically outside Skyreach's territory but entirely dependent on it — most of its economy runs on selling monster parts brought down from the aerie. The closest thing Skyreach has to an embassy to the outside world.

**The Shrine of First Feather** — A quiet, wind-carved alcove near the village's highest point, dedicated to the celestial patron the Five Houses trace their blood to. Most Skyreach Aasimar visit before their Long Flight, whether or not they consider themselves devout — it's less about faith and more about tradition, and no one wants to be the one who skipped it before something went wrong.

## Culture Notes

- **Sky burial** is standard practice — the dead are carried to the highest point a House can manage and released to the wind rather than buried in the ground, which Skyreach's people consider a strange and slightly unsettling lowland custom.
- Rivalry between Houses is real but rarely violent — mostly expressed through boasting rights after a hunt, and the size of what each House's hunters add to the Bone Spire.
- Companions are treated as family, not tools. Losing one is grieved the same as losing a person, and a hunter who loses their companion and refuses to bond with another is quietly, permanently pitied by the rest of the village.
- Skyreach measures a hunter's worth almost entirely by what they've brought down, not by rank or birth — which is exactly the system Kass is trying to use to make a name for himself faster than tradition usually allows.
